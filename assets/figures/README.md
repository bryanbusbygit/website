# Source figure library

Raster source figures in this directory are crops from the cited sources.
Surrounding figure numbers, captions, and slide text have been removed so
each image contains only the drawing. Editable SVG redraws are identified below.

## Laubenbacher and Pareigis, 2001

Source: R. Laubenbacher and B. Pareigis, “Equivalence Relations on Finite
Dynamical Systems,” Advances in Applied Mathematics 26 (2001), 237–251.
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1006/aama.2000.0717).

- `laubenbacher-pareigis-state-spaces.png` — Figure 1 on printed p. 250
  (PDF p. 14), cropped to the eighteen numbered drawings. Rendered directly
  from the PDF at 3 pixels per point; the graph artwork is unchanged.
- The source omits self-loops at fixed points. The caption explains this.
- These are general two-variable state spaces, not eighteen linear systems.
  The accompanying discussion in Section 4 identifies six linear types.
- This figure is from Laubenbacher and Pareigis, not Elspas. An accessible
  full-text copy of Elspas's 1959 article was not available during this edit.

## Original explanatory diagrams

- `primitive-matrix-powers.svg` shows the support of powers 1, 5, and 6
  of the dependency matrix for `(x2, x1*x3, x4, x2)`. Rows are destinations
  and columns are sources. Integer matrix multiplication gives 5, 15, and
  16 positive entries respectively. At power 5 the only zero is (3, 4).
- `frobenius-gaps.svg` marks membership in the semigroup generated by 4
  and 7 on a single positive-integer number line, showing 1 through 20
  with continuation to the right. Its largest gap is 17.
- `arithmetical-subdivision.svg` illustrates the local subdivision rule.
  Inserting r-label a+b gives d-label 1 and increases both endpoint
  d-labels by 1. Dotted extensions represent unchanged incidences.

These SVGs were drawn for the essays from the stated mathematical data.
They are not reproductions of literature figures. The subdivision replaces
the doubled-path illustration in the arithmetical essay, and the matrix
powers replace the acyclic-subgraph illustration in the Boolean essay.
The earlier drawings remain available in the source figure library.
