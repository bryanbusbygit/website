Boolean Monomial Dynamics

We study the maximum transient length of Boolean monomial dynamical systems. Dependencies in successive iterates correspond to directed walks, allowing transient lengths to be computed from a graph on the variables. Our results concern families for which the cycle lengths and their intersections give exact formulas.

Definition 1 (Boolean dynamical system)

A Boolean dynamical system is a map \(f:\{0,1\}^n\to\{0,1\}^n\). All coordinates are updated simultaneously at each iteration.

The state graph has edges \(a\to f(a)\) and consists of periodic orbits with directed trees attached. We write \(t(f)\) for the maximum number of iterations needed to reach a periodic state, taken over all initial states.

Computing \(t(f)\) from the state graph requires up to \(2^n\) states. For monomial maps, iteration only changes which variables occur in each coordinate product. Tracking those variables leads to a dependency graph on \(n\) vertices.

Definition 2 (Boolean monomial system)

A Boolean system is monomial if each coordinate function is a product of selected variables. We exclude identically zero coordinate functions.

Since \(a_j^2=a_j\), the monomials may be taken squarefree. The empty product is \(1\).

The dependency graph \(D_f\) has an edge \(j\to i\) when \(a_j\) occurs in \(f_i\). Walks in this graph describe dependencies in the iterates of \(f\).

Theorem 1 (Dependencies and directed walks)

For a Boolean monomial system with nonzero squarefree coordinates and \(m\geq1\), the variable \(a_j\) occurs in \((f^m)_i\) if and only if a directed walk of length \(m\) joins \(j\) to \(i\) in \(D_f\) [3].

The adjacency matrix \(A\), with rows indexing destinations, records dependencies in one iteration. Its powers record dependencies in successive iterates. Because a single zero factor makes a monomial vanish, these dependencies determine where initial zeros occur at later times.

Lemma 1 (Propagation of zeros)

Under the hypotheses of Theorem 1, \((f^m(a))_i=0\) if and only if some vertex \(j\) with \(a_j=0\) has a directed walk of length \(m\) to \(i\).

To obtain a time by which every initial zero has affected every coordinate, we need walks of a common length between every ordered pair of vertices. Strong connectivity supplies paths, but their lengths need not agree. Traversing cycles extends these lengths, and the gcd of the cycle lengths determines whether a common length is eventually possible.

A strongly connected digraph is primitive when its cycle lengths have gcd \(1\). Its exponent \(\gamma(D_f)\) is the least power of \(A\) with all entries positive [7].

Theorem 2 (Transient length and exponent)

Let \(f\) be a Boolean monomial system with nonzero squarefree coordinates in \(n\geq2\) variables. If \(D_f\) is primitive, its exponent equals the maximum transient length [5].

\[ t(f)=\gamma(D_f). \]

At the exponent, every coordinate depends on every initial variable. Every state containing a zero has therefore reached the zero fixed point. The theorem shows that this time is attained, so estimating the exponent determines the maximal transient.

Computing successive matrix powers gives the exponent, but does not explain its dependence on the graph. Cycle lengths offer another approach. Inserting a traversal of a cycle extends a walk without changing its endpoints, so sums of cycle lengths describe the increments obtainable in this way.

For cycle lengths \(c_1,\ldots,c_k\) with gcd \(1\), their nonnegative integer combinations form the numerical semigroup \(\langle c_1,\ldots,c_k\rangle\) [4]. Its largest missing integer is the Frobenius number \(\mathrm{ℊ}\), with \(\mathrm{ℊ}=-1\) when there are no gaps.

Theorem 3 (Sylvester's formula)

If \(a,b>1\) are coprime integers (their gcd is \(1\)), the largest integer outside \(\langle a,b\rangle\) is [8]

\[ \mathrm{ℊ}=ab-a-b. \]

Above the Frobenius number, every integer is a sum of cycle lengths. This does not yet produce walks between prescribed endpoints, since the cycles in a chosen sum may be at different locations. The 2024 poster [6] proposes using the directed diameter to bound the additional travel.

Proposed bounds (Diameter and Frobenius number)

Let \(f\) be a Boolean monomial system with nonzero squarefree coordinates and primitive dependency graph \(D_f\). Write \(d=\operatorname{diam}(D_f)\), and let \(\mathrm{ℊ}\) be the Frobenius number of the semigroup generated by its cycle lengths. The poster aims to establish, under suitable hypotheses on the graph, the lower and upper bounds

\[ \begin{aligned} 1+\mathrm{ℊ}+d&\leq t(f),\\ t(f)&\leq \mathrm{ℊ}+2d. \end{aligned} \]

Primitivity alone does not imply these bounds. Additional hypotheses must account for the locations of the cycles used to realize a given length.

Families with exact transient formulas

The difficulty of reaching different cycles is simpler for a bouquet, where all cycles meet at one vertex. This allows an exact formula. Our manuscript in preparation also treats an arrangement with two intersection vertices, where travel between the intersections contributes to the transient. Coordinate functions remain nonzero throughout.

A cycle bouquet consists of directed cycles meeting at one shared vertex, with no other intersections. Let their lengths be \(2\leq c_1<\cdots<c_k=L\), with gcd \(1\), and let \(\mathrm{ℊ}\) be their Frobenius number.

Theorem 4 (Cycle bouquets)

If \(D_f\) is a cycle bouquet with the lengths and hypotheses above, then

\[ t(f)=\mathrm{ℊ}+2L-1. \]

For two coprime lengths \(p<q\), Sylvester’s formula makes the bouquet result explicit, giving \(t(f)=pq-p+q-1\).

The second family joins three directed cycles of lengths \(2\leq p<q<r\) at two shared vertices. The \(p\)- and \(q\)-cycles meet only at \(u\), and the \(q\)- and \(r\)-cycles meet only at \(v\), with no other intersections. On the \(q\)-cycle, the directed segment from \(u\) to \(v\) has length \(q-1\), followed by the edge \(v\to u\). Denote this graph by \(G(p,q,r)\).

Theorem 5 (Three cycles joined at two vertices)

Suppose \(D_f=G(p,q,r)\), \(\gcd(p,q)=1\), and \(r-q\in\langle p,q\rangle\). Thus \(r-q\) is a nonnegative integer combination of \(p\) and \(q\). With \(\mathrm{ℊ}=pq-p-q\),

\[ \begin{aligned} t(f)&=\mathrm{ℊ}+q+2r-1\\ &=pq-p+2r-1. \end{aligned} \]

For lengths \(2,3,5\), the bouquet has transient \(10\) and the two-vertex arrangement above has transient \(13\). Thus the transient depends on the intersection pattern even when the cycle lengths are fixed.

Open questions

Extending the exact formulas requires accounting for the walks between cycles as well as the semigroup generated by their lengths. The hypotheses needed for the proposed diameter bounds remain undetermined.
