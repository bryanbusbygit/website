Arithmetical Structures on Graphs

Lorenzini introduced arithmetical graphs in connection with intersection matrices arising from degenerating algebraic curves. [1] An arithmetical structure labels the vertices of a graph with positive integers that satisfy a divisibility rule based on adjacency. We study how many such structures occur on cycles with one multi-edge. Our approach combines smoothing, subdivision counts given by ballot numbers, and a constructive description of the arithmetic data at the multi-edge.

Definitions and enumeration

Let \(G=(V,E)\) be a loopless multigraph, with vertex set \(V\) and edge multiset \(E\). Write \(uv\in E\) when \(u\) and \(v\) are adjacent, and let \(m(u,v)\) count the copies of that edge. An arithmetical structure on \(G\) assigns a positive integer \(r(v)\) to each vertex such that \(r(v)\) divides the sum of the adjacent labels, with parallel edges counted separately. We call the resulting positive integer quotient \(d(v)\).

Multiplying every label by the same positive integer preserves the divisibility conditions, so we impose the normalization \(\gcd\{r(v):v\in V\}=1\). Write \(\mathbf r=(r(v))_{v\in V}\) for the vector of labels and \(\mathbf d=(d(v))_{v\in V}\) for the quotients. Together they form an arithmetical structure, whose defining equation is

\[ (\operatorname{diag}\mathbf d-A_G)\mathbf r=0. \]

Here \(\operatorname{diag}\mathbf d\) is the diagonal matrix whose entry at vertex \(v\) is \(d(v)\), and \(A_G=(m(v,u))_{v,u\in V}\) is the adjacency matrix of \(G\). The corresponding equation at a vertex \(v\) is

\[ d(v)r(v)=\sum_{u\in V}m(v,u)r(u), \]

The terms for nonadjacent vertices vanish because \(m(v,u)=0\). Therefore, \(\mathbf r\) uniquely determines \(\mathbf d\), and the enumeration reduces to counting primitive positive integer vectors \(\mathbf r\) for which all the resulting quotients are positive integers.

Braun et al. enumerated arithmetical structures on paths and cycles. [2] For a cycle on \(n\) vertices, the number of structures is

\[ \binom{2n-1}{n-1}. \]

Let \(\widetilde C_{n,k}\) denote the multigraph obtained by replacing one edge of an \(n\)-cycle by \(k\) parallel edges, where \(k\geq2\). At each endpoint of the multi-edge, the label at the other endpoint appears \(k\) times in the adjacency sum. The resulting divisibility conditions depend on \(k\), requiring an extension of the ordinary cycle enumeration. Glass and Wagner studied the analogous enumeration problem for paths with a doubled edge. [3]

Reduction by smoothing

Smoothing reduces the underlying graph while preserving an arithmetical structure. Suppose a vertex \(v\) away from the multi-edge has neighbors \(u\) and \(w\) and satisfies \(d(v)=1\). The vertex equation gives \(r(v)=r(u)+r(w)\). Deleting \(v\), joining \(u\) and \(w\), and adjusting their \(d\)-labels yields an arithmetical structure on the smaller graph.

The inverse operation subdivides an edge and assigns the new vertex the sum of its endpoint labels. We can therefore organize the count by reduced structures and the subdivisions allowed for each one. Our reduction expresses the total count in terms of strongly smooth arithmetical structures, with subdivision multiplicities given by ballot numbers. The precise reduction conditions and the definition of strong smoothness are given in the current manuscript.

The arithmetic constraints at the multi-edge require a separate analysis. Let \(r_1\) and \(r_2\) be the \(r\)-labels at its endpoints. The equations there force \(r_1\), \(r_2\), and \(k\) to be pairwise coprime, meaning that each pair has greatest common divisor \(1\). The construction construction below proves the converse: it produces a structure from any data that satisfies this condition.

Construction and enumeration

For positive integers \(r_1,r_2,k\), pairwise coprimality is both necessary and sufficient for the triple to occur at the multi-edge of an arithmetical structure. A modified Euclidean algorithm proves sufficiency by constructing a structure with the chosen endpoint labels and multiplicity.

The number of structures on \(\widetilde C_{n,k}\) is expressed as a weighted sum over strongly smooth structures on at most \(n\) vertices. Each weight counts the subdivisions contributing to a structure on the original graph. The complete formula and the construction appear in the current manuscript.

The rightmost example illustrates the divisibility condition. The vertex labeled \(7\) has four edges to the vertex labeled \(5\) and one edge to the vertex labeled \(1\). Its neighboring labels contribute \(4\cdot5+1=21\), so its \(d\)-label is \(3\). The remaining vertex equations similarly determine the displayed \(\mathbf d\)-labeling.
